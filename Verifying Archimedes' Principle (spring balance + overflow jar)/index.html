Understanding Archimedes' Principle

Have you noticed that a stone feels much lighter when you lift it underwater compared to lifting it in air? Water is secretly pushing it upward — this upward push is what Archimedes' Principle explains!

🔍 What is actually happening here?

When an object is dipped in a liquid, the liquid pushes it upward with a force called the upthrust or buoyant force. This is why objects feel lighter in water.

Archimedes' Principle states that when a solid is fully or partly immersed in a liquid, it experiences an upward buoyant force that is exactly equal to the weight of the liquid it displaces (pushes out of the way).

So if we measure the weight of the solid in air, then its weight in water, the "loss in weight" should exactly match the weight of the water that overflowed out of a completely full jar when the solid was dipped in it.

Loss of weight in water = Weight of liquid displaced This equality is what we verify in this experiment
Real Life Example: This is exactly why huge steel ships float on water (they displace a huge weight of water, so the upthrust balances their weight), and why it's much easier to lift a friend in a swimming pool than on land!
🏡
Verify It Yourself
📋 Material Required for this experiment:
🪨
A Small Stone or Metal Bolt
⚖️
A Spring Balance
🫙
A Jar Filled Right to the Brim
🥤
An Empty Collecting Cup

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Tie the stone to the spring balance and note its weight in air (W₁).
Step 2: Fill the overflow jar with water right up to the spout, and place an empty, pre-weighed cup under the spout to catch any overflow.
Step 3: Slowly lower the stone (still hanging from the spring balance) completely into the jar, without letting it touch the bottom or sides. Water will overflow out of the spout into the cup below.
Step 4: With the stone still submerged, note the new spring balance reading — the weight in water (W₂). Loss of weight = W₁ − W₂.
Step 5: Now weigh the cup with the collected overflow water. You'll find this weight is almost exactly equal to the loss of weight (W₁ − W₂) — verifying Archimedes' Principle!

Quick Summary Points
1
Upthrust Exists: A liquid always pushes an immersed object upward with a force called upthrust (buoyant force).
2
Archimedes' Principle: The buoyant force equals the weight of the liquid displaced by the immersed object.
3
Loss of Weight = Displaced Weight: Weight in air minus weight in water equals the weight of the overflowed liquid.
4
Explains Floating: Ships and boats float because they displace enough water for the upthrust to balance their weight.
