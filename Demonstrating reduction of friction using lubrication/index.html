Reducing Friction Using Lubrication
Easy Science Class 8
📖
Understanding Lubrication

Have you heard a door hinge that "creaks" loudly, and then someone puts a drop of oil on it and it goes silent? That oil is doing something clever — it's reducing friction!

🔍 What is actually happening here?

We already know that friction happens because tiny bumps on two touching surfaces rub and lock against each other.

A lubricant is a slippery substance — like oil, grease, or even water — that we put between two rubbing surfaces.

The lubricant fills up the tiny gaps and bumps, forming a thin slippery layer. Now the two rough surfaces don't touch each other directly anymore — instead, they slide over this smooth liquid layer. This makes it much easier to slide, and greatly reduces the friction.

This process of adding a lubricant to reduce friction is called lubrication.

Real Life Example: That is why mechanics oil a bicycle chain, machines in factories are greased regularly, and cooks add a little oil to a frying pan — all to reduce friction and make movement smoother!
🏡
The Oiled Surface Test
📋 Material Required for this experiment:
📦
A Small Box or Toy Car
🪵
A Flat Wooden Board
🫗
A Few Drops of Cooking Oil
🧻
Tissue or Cloth (to spread it)

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Place the box on the dry wooden board and push it gently with one finger. Notice how much force it takes to start it sliding.
Step 2: Now, put a few drops of cooking oil on the board and spread it evenly with a tissue or cloth to make a thin, slippery layer.
Step 3: Place the same box on the oiled part of the board and give it the exact same gentle push.
Step 4: Compare — on the oiled surface, the box slides much farther and more easily than on the dry surface. This proves that the oil (lubricant) reduced the friction between the box and the board!

Quick Summary Points
1
Lubricant = Slippery Helper: Oil, grease, or water placed between two rubbing surfaces to reduce friction.
2
Fills the Bumps: The lubricant coats the tiny bumps so the rough surfaces no longer touch and lock directly.
3
Oiled = Easier Sliding: With less friction, the same push force makes objects slide farther and more smoothly.
4
Used Everywhere: Oiling bicycle chains, greasing machine gears, and cooking with oil are everyday examples of lubrication.
