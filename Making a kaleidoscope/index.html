Understanding a Kaleidoscope

Ever looked into a kaleidoscope and seen a beautiful, perfectly symmetric pattern that changes every time you twist it? That magic is simply light bouncing again and again between mirrors — a idea called multiple reflection.

Number of Images (n) = 360° ÷ Angle between mirrors
Smaller angle → more mirrors images → busier pattern

🔍 What is actually happening here?

Inside a kaleidoscope, two or three long mirror strips are fixed at an angle to each other, forming a narrow tunnel (usually a triangular prism).

When light enters, it reflects from one mirror to the next, over and over. Each reflection creates one more copy of the image, and because the mirrors are arranged symmetrically, all these copies line up into a neat, radial, snowflake-like pattern.

At one end, loose colourful beads or glass pieces are placed. Every time you rotate that end, the beads fall into new positions, and the mirrors instantly turn that random jumble into a brand-new symmetric design.

Real Life Example: A kaleidoscope, a barber shop's two facing mirrors "long tunnel" effect, and the sparkly patterns in some jewellery photo filters all use the same idea of multiple reflection between mirrors!

Build Your Own Kaleidoscope
📋 Material Required for this activity:
📜
A Cardboard Tube
🪞
3 Rectangular Mirror Strips
💎
Colourful Beads / Glass Pieces
📄
Butter Paper (Frosted)
🔍
Clear Plastic Sheet
✂️
Tape, Scissors, Rubber Band

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Cut the three mirror strips to the same length as the tube and tape their edges together, reflective side inward, to form a long triangular prism.
Step 2: Slide this mirror-prism snugly inside the cardboard tube.
Step 3: At one end, poke a small peep hole in a paper cap and fix it — this is where you'll look through.
Step 4: At the other end, place a clear plastic sheet, sprinkle a few colourful beads or glass pieces on it, and cover with a frosted butter-paper disc so light spreads evenly. Fix this loose enough to rotate with a rubber band.
Step 5: Point the tube toward light, look through the peep hole, and gently twist the bead end — watch the dazzling symmetric pattern change with every turn!

Quick Summary Points
1
Multiple Reflection: A kaleidoscope works because light reflects again and again between angled mirrors.
2
Mirror Arrangement: Usually 3 mirror strips are joined into a triangular prism and slid inside a tube.
3
Formula: Number of images n = 360° ÷ angle between the mirrors.
4
Colourful Chamber: Loose beads or glass pieces at one end, between a clear sheet and frosted paper, create the coloured shapes.
5
Ever-Changing Pattern: Every twist shifts the beads randomly, and the mirrors turn that jumble into a fresh symmetric design.
