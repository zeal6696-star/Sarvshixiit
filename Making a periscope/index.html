Understanding a Periscope

Ever seen how a soldier peeks over a wall, or a submarine sees ships on the surface while staying underwater? They use a simple device called a periscope, which lets you see things that are above your eye level or hidden behind an obstacle.

Both mirrors fixed at 45° to the tube
Light bends twice, by 90° each time, to reach your eye

🔍 What is actually happening here?

A periscope has two plane mirrors fixed parallel to each other, each tilted at 45° to the tube, one at the top and one at the bottom.

Light from the object first strikes the top mirror and reflects downward through the tube by 90°. This reflected ray then hits the bottom mirror, which reflects it again by 90°, sending it straight into your eye.

Because of this double reflection, you can see over tall obstacles or around corners without exposing yourself — this is exactly the principle used in submarine periscopes and tank periscopes.

Real Life Example: Submarines use long periscopes to view ships and the sky while staying underwater, and soldiers use small periscopes to see over trench walls without being seen themselves!

Build Your Own Periscope
📋 Material Required for this activity:
📦
A Long Cardboard Box / Tetra Pack
🪞
2 Small Plane Mirrors
✂️
Scissors / Cutter
📐
Ruler and Pencil
🧻
Tape or Glue

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Take the long box and mark two slits, one near the top and one near the bottom, on opposite faces, each cut at a 45° angle.
Step 2: Also cut a small viewing hole in front of the bottom slit, and a small opening for light to enter in front of the top slit.
Step 3: Slide one mirror into the top slit, tilted at 45°, facing the opening above it.
Step 4: Slide the second mirror into the bottom slit, tilted at 45° and parallel to the first mirror, facing the viewing hole.
Step 5: Fix both mirrors firmly with tape, close up the box, and look through the bottom hole while pointing the top opening over a wall — you should see whatever is on the other side!

Quick Summary Points
1
Purpose: A periscope lets us see objects above our eye level or hidden behind obstacles.
2
Two Mirrors at 45°: Both mirrors are fixed parallel to each other, each tilted 45° to the tube.
3
Double Reflection: Light bends 90° at the top mirror, travels down the tube, then bends another 90° at the bottom mirror into the eye.
4
Parallel Mirrors: Because the mirrors are parallel, the final image travels in the same direction as the original light, just shifted.
5
Used Everywhere: Submarines, tanks, and trench-viewing devices all use periscopes based on this same idea.
