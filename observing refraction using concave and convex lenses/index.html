What does a Lens do to Light?

When light passes through a clean glass window, it goes straight. But if the glass is special and curved, it bends the light beams! This bending of light when it passes through a transparent object is called Refraction.

🔍 What is actually happening here?

Lenses are transparent glasses with curved surfaces that bend light passing through them. There are two main types:

1. Convex Lens (The Joiner): This lens is thick in the middle and thin at the borders. When light passes through it, it bends all the rays inwards to meet at one single point. That's why it can make small text look giant! It is also called a Converging Lens.

2. Concave Lens (The Divider): This lens is thin in the middle and thick at the edges. When light goes through it, it stretches and scatters the rays wide apart. It makes things look tiny and cute! It is also called a Diverging Lens.

Sunbeam Magic: Since a convex lens bends all light rays into one tight spot, you can use it as a magnifying glass under the sun to create a super bright, hot dot of light on a paper sheet!

The Simple Lens Text Test
📋 Material Required for this experiment:
🔍
1 Standard Convex Lens (Magnifying Glass)
👓
1 Concave Lens (Short-sight Spectacles)
📖
1 Book with small printed letters

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Open any book or newspaper and look closely at the small text.
Step 2: Hold the Convex Lens over the letters and slowly move it away from the page. Notice how the alphabets swell up and look huge and easy to read!
Step 3: Now, replace it with the Concave Lens and look at the same text. Wow! The alphabets look extremely tiny, sharp, and drifted far away.

Quick Summary Points
1
Refraction Meaning: Refraction is just a scientific name for the bending of light rays when entering a transparent material.
2
Convex Joins: Convex lenses converge light into a small bright dot and magnify book characters.
3
Concave Splits: Concave lenses diverge or spread out incoming light rays, making objects appear shrunken.
