Dispersion of White Light Using a Prism
Easy Science Class 7
📖
The Hidden Secrets of White Light

Sunlight and ordinary light from lamps appear completely white to our eyes. However, white light is actually a magnificent combination of seven different beautiful colors hidden perfectly inside it!

🔍 What is actually happening here?

When a narrow beam of sunlight or a direct white light beam passes through a triangular piece of glass called a Glass Prism, the light bends at different angles.

Because each of the seven constituent colors travels at a different speed inside the glass, they bend by different amounts and separate from one another. This splitting of white light into its component colors is called Dispersion.

The colorful band of seven colors that forms on the screen is known as a Spectrum. You can easily remember the exact order of these colors using the acronym: VIBGYOR (Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, and Red).

The Secret of Rainbows: Right after a rain shower, tiny water droplets suspended in the air act like microscopic prisms. When sunlight passes through these floating droplets, it splits into seven colors, forming a beautiful rainbow in the sky!
🏡
Catching a Home Rainbow
📋 Material Required for this experiment:
📐
1 Glass Prism (Equilateral)
🔦
1 Bright Torch or Sunlight Beam
📄
1 Plain White Sheet or Wall

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Make your room dark, or stand near a window where a thin beam of direct sunlight is entering.
Step 2: Hold the glass prism in your hand so that the beam of white light or torch light falls straight onto one of its flat faces.
Step 3: Slowly rotate the prism until the light emerging from the opposite side falls directly onto an adjacent White Paper Sheet or Wall. You will see a beautiful band of seven vibrant colors painting the surface!

Quick Summary Points
1
Dispersion Rule: White light splits up into 7 individual distinct colors when entering a thick angled glass structure.
2
VIBGYOR Sequence: The colors spread from bottom to top in the exact order of Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, and Red.
3
Nature's Analogy: Rainbows are formed due to dispersion of sunlight by countless tiny floating water drops acting as micro prisms.
