Measuring Density of a Solid
Spring Balance + Measuring Cylinder Method
📖
Understanding Density

Ever wondered why a small iron nail sinks in water but a big plastic bottle floats? It's all about how much mass is packed into a given space — this property is called density.

Density (ρ) = Mass (m) ÷ Volume (V)
Usually measured in g/cm³ or g/mL

🔍 How do we measure it for a solid?

To find density we need two things: the mass of the solid and its volume.

Mass is found by hanging the solid from a spring balance and reading its weight directly in grams.

Volume of an irregular solid can't be measured with a ruler, so we use a measuring cylinder filled with water. We note the water level before (V₁) and after (V₂) fully dipping the solid in. The solid pushes the water level up by an amount exactly equal to its own volume, so Volume = V₂ − V₁.

Why this works: A solid dipped fully in water displaces (pushes aside) a volume of water exactly equal to its own volume. This simple idea is called the displacement method and lets us measure the volume of even oddly shaped stones or metal pieces.
🏡
Find the Density of a Stone
📋 Material Required for this experiment:
🪨
A Small Stone or Metal Piece
⚖️
A Spring Balance
🧵
A Piece of Thread
🧪
A Measuring Cylinder
💧
Some Water

🚀 What to do? (Steps):

Step 1: Tie the thread around the stone and hang it from the hook of the spring balance. Note the reading — this is the mass (m) of the stone in grams.
Step 2: Pour some water into the measuring cylinder and note the initial water level — this is V₁.
Step 3: Gently lower the stone into the water using the thread until it is fully submerged, without touching the bottom or sides. Note the new water level — this is V₂.
Step 4: Calculate the volume of the stone as V₂ − V₁, then find density using Density = Mass ÷ Volume.

Quick Summary Points
1
Density Formula: Density = Mass ÷ Volume, usually expressed in g/cm³ or g/mL.
2
Mass from Spring Balance: Hang the solid using a thread and read its mass directly off the spring balance.
3
Volume by Displacement: Note water level before (V₁) and after (V₂) fully dipping the solid; Volume = V₂ − V₁.
4
Works for Irregular Shapes: This displacement method is especially useful for stones or objects whose shape makes ruler measurement impossible.
5
Compare with Water: Water has a density of 1 g/cm³. Anything with a higher density than water sinks; anything lower floats.
