Magnetic vs Non-Magnetic

Magnets are amazing objects that exert an invisible pulling force. But they are picky! They do not pull every object in the room. Materials are divided into two main groups:

• Magnetic Materials: Materials that get strongly attracted towards a magnet. Iron, Nickel, and Cobalt are the most famous magnetic elements.

• Non-Magnetic Materials: Materials that are completely ignored by magnets and show absolutely no attraction. Examples include wood, paper, plastic, leather, and aluminum.
💡
Lodestone: Nature's Magnet

Long ago, shepherds discovered a natural magnetic rock called lodestone (magnetite ore). Today, we shape artificial magnets into cool forms like Bar magnets, Horseshoe magnets, and Cylindrical magnets for everyday gadgets!

Comprehensive Lab Summary Notes
• The Rule of Magnetic Attraction: Magnets generate an invisible force field that loops around them. This force specifically pulls items made of iron, steel, nickel, or cobalt. Whenever these items come close, they fly toward the magnet poles and stick to them.
• The Nature of Non-Magnetic Elements: A wide variety of substances around us completely ignore magnetic fields. Materials like wood, plastics, school erasers, leather shoes, or glass mirrors are unaffected because their internal atom clusters do not line up with magnetic forces.
• Separation Trick: Knowing this magnetic difference is highly useful in everyday waste management and industries! Giant electromagnets are hung over messy garbage disposal piles to instantly pick out metal scrap rods and iron cans away from non-magnetic plastics, rags, and paper rubbish.
• Metal Confusion Warning: Do not assume that ALL metals are magnetic! While iron and steel coins jump toward magnets instantly, metals like aluminum soda cans, copper plumbing pipes, and gold rings are completely non-magnetic.
